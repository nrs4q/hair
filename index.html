About the Film

Hair is a psychological Southern Gothic arthouse horror film focused on atmosphere, character, symbolism, and emotional realism rather than spectacle. The story explores themes of loneliness, secrecy, aging, addiction, and the hidden rituals individuals create to cope with isolation.


The project contains no explicit sexuality, no nudity, no graphic gore, no political agenda, no religious messaging, and no extreme violence. The emphasis is psychological tension and mood.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why this film? +
This film grew from years of discussion between brothers who turned imagination into something real.

Blood Brothers is a grassroots independent film studio driven by creativity, ambition, and a deep love for storytelling and cinema. What started as conversation evolved into a focused psychological short film with aspirations for film festivals and maybe something bigger.

Supporting this project means backing local artists creating thoughtful cinema. It means helping bring years of dreaming, creativity, and dedication to the screen.
What makes the project unique? +
Hair emerges from a distinctly personal and artistic voice shaped through years of photography, literature, philosophy, music, and visual storytelling. Writer-director Zack Smith’s work is characterized by concise yet symbolically rich writing that prioritizes mood, psychological tension, implication, and emotional atmosphere over exposition. His storytelling often explores loneliness, ritual, memory, secrecy, and the quiet contradictions within ordinary people.

Rather than relying on spectacle, Hair builds unease through imagery, silence, behavior, and recurring visual motifs. The film blends southern gothic atmosphere with psychologically grounded characters and a strong sense of place, creating something both intimate and unsettling.

The project is also unique because it is rooted deeply within the creative identity of a small Tennessee town rarely associated with independent arthouse filmmaking. Real locations, regional textures, and local experiences shape the world of the film in an authentic way that cannot be manufactured artificially.

At the same time, the production brings together an unexpected collaboration of artists: brothers from a small town working alongside established Nashville creatives and visual media professionals to create ambitious independent cinema outside traditional industry systems.

Hair is ultimately the result of years of artistic development, collaboration, networking, and imagination converging into a film that feels handmade, personal, regional, and visually unique.
What audience is the film intended for? +
Audiences who appreciate independent cinema, psychological horror, Southern Gothic storytelling, arthouse filmmaking, literary themes, and symbollically rich, visually driven narratives.
Will this horror film be profitable? +
Not necessarily — and profitability was never the central reason this project exists.

Hair began as an artistic and intellectual exercise between brothers driven by imagination, visual storytelling, literature, psychology, and a shared desire to create something meaningful together. The film represents years of creative energy slowly taking shape through writing, photography, filmmaking, and collaboration.

While we absolutely hope the film resonates with audiences and succeeds within festivals and independent film circles, the true value of the project lies in the process itself: learning how to build a film from the ground up, discovering our creative voice, collaborating with a creative team we admire, and realizing an idea that has lived in our imaginations for years.

Even beyond the finished product, the experience, relationships, artistic growth, and knowledge gained through making the film will be invaluable.
Where is the film being produced? +
Production is based in Shelbyville, Tennessee and developed through a network of local artists, creatives, filmmakers, and collaborators throughout Middle Tennessee.
What is the long-term vision? +
Blood Brothers aims to create thoughtful independent cinema that explores film, literature, psychology, philosophy, and visual symbolism through deeply personal storytelling.

About Blood Brothers

Blood Brothers is a grassroots independent film project created by four brothers and longtime friends from rural Tennessee. The collective exists to create meaningful art through collaboration, friendship, craftsmanship, and storytelling.


Rather than pursuing spectacle or controversy, the project focuses on emotional realism, atmosphere, literature, psychology, philosophy, memory, and visual symbolism. Every aspect of production embraces a handmade approach rooted in local talent and authentic creative passion.
